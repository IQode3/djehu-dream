# Books that stay out

The judged pair is already chosen. It is in `docs/research/data-slice.md`: a dream note we write, one short passage from the 1913 Brill Freud, and one short passage from a public-domain Aristotle. This file is the list of Jung and von Franz books that are not stored.

"Publicly available" splits in two. A book you can borrow, stream, or find as a PDF is not a book you can copy into this repo. A book that is public domain in the United States can still be in copyright in the EU. Jung died in 1961. In countries that use life plus seventy years, his own writings generally stay protected through 2031. This project is being written in Austria. Project Gutenberg's US public-domain mark is not a European clearance.

## What can be read as a US public-domain text

| Text | What it is | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| C. G. Jung, *Psychology of the Unconscious*, translated by Beatrice M. Hinkle (1916). Project Gutenberg ebook 65903. Also on Wikisource. | Jung's early book on fantasy, myth, and symbol. He reads one woman's fantasies against myths. | Gutenberg says public domain in the United States, and tells readers outside the US to check local law. This is not the later *Symbols of Transformation* (Collected Works 5, 1952), which is a rewrite and is still in copyright. It is also not a dream manual. It is one long symbolic reading. |

That is the one substantial English text in this circle that is clearly offered as US public domain. It is not a corpus of analyzed dreams from the seminar years.

## The dream books people mean, still in copyright

These are the real dream-analysis corpus. They are published, sold, and often borrowable. Borrowing is not a license to store the text here.

| Text | Why it is the one people remember |
|---|---|
| C. G. Jung, *Dream Analysis: Notes of the Seminar Given in 1928–1930*, edited by William McGuire (Princeton / Routledge, 1984) | The long teaching seminar. About thirty dreams of one man, discussed across two years. The notes existed earlier as a private Zurich transcript (second edition 1938, reprinted privately in 1958 and 1972). The public book is copyright 1984. Internet Archive holds a copy for borrowing. It is not a free text. |
| C. G. Jung, *Children's Dreams*, and the other seminar volumes issued later by the Philemon Foundation with Princeton | Old notes, new editions. The publication date is recent. The estate still holds them. |
| Collected Works 8, including "General Aspects of Dream Psychology" and "On the Nature of Dreams" | The short statements of his dream theory. The Collected Works translations and revisions are in copyright. |
| Collected Works 5, *Symbols of Transformation* | The 1952 replacement of the 1916 book. Copyrighted. Do not treat it as the Gutenberg text. |
| Collected Works 12, *Psychology and Alchemy* | Dream series used as alchemical images. Copyrighted. |

## His students

Marie-Louise von Franz is the student who wrote the practice this project names. Her dream books are late twentieth century. They are in copyright. *The Way of the Dream* is the film and book named as the reason. No sentence from it is stored. *Dreams* (Shambhala, 1998) and *The Interpretation of Fairy Tales* are the same situation: you can buy them, and some copies sit on the Internet Archive as borrowable scans. That does not make a corpus.

The same is true of the wider circle. Barbara Hannah, Emma Jung, Jolande Jacobi, Aniela Jaffé, Erich Neumann, Edward Edinger, and James Hillman have books a reader can buy. None of those books are a public-domain corpus of dream analyses.

## What not to mistake for a corpus

- A PDF of a Princeton or Shambhala book. Availability is not permission.
- An Internet Archive page marked "borrow" or "print-disabled." That is a loan.
- A short credit is not a license to store the book.
- The 1916 Gutenberg text used as if it were the Collected Works. Jung changed that book.

## For the conversation, not a decision

The shelf people hope is public is mostly the 1928–1930 seminar and von Franz's dream books. Those are the right books to read. They are the wrong books to paste into the repo. The text that is actually offered as public domain in the United States is the 1916 *Psychology of the Unconscious*, and even that needs a European check before any excerpt is committed. It is not the judged pair.

## How people become Jungian analysts

There is no database of analyzed dreams, and the gap is not an accident. The International Association for Analytical Psychology certifies analysts through institutes. A typical program is several years of three things at once: your own analysis, several hundred hours of it, supervision of your work with clients, and seminars on the Collected Works. Chicago's program describes about 700 hours of class, exams, and a thesis. Los Angeles and New York require analysis with an IAAP analyst before you may even apply, and analysis continues through training. Client dreams stay confidential. They are not uploaded.

What trainees read is the Collected Works, the seminar books, and the institute library. New York includes the Kristine Mann Library and a membership to ARAS in its tuition. That is a bookshelf and a paid image archive, not an open API.

## What can be taken

| Source | What you get | What you do not get |
|---|---|---|
| Gutenberg 65903, the 1916 Hinkle translation | A US public-domain text of Jung's early symbolic reading. Possible excerpts only after a European copyright check. | The dream seminar. The Collected Works. A clearance for Austria. |
| ARAS Online, [aras.org](https://aras.org/) | About 18,000 images and commentary, searchable by a symbol. Membership is about $100 a year, less for some students. Images may be used in unpublished, non-commercial work under their agreement. | An open API. A right to copy the commentary into a repo. A judge who can run the product without a membership. |
| Sleep and Dream Database (SDDb), and the older Dreambank | Thousands of dream reports, open to search. This is the empirical side, close to Hall and Van de Castle. | Jungian interpretations. A meaning for a symbol. |
| A title named in the other notes | A way to find the book. | The book itself. |

ARAS is the closest thing to the database you expected. It is a member search of pictures and notes. It is not a feed of Jung's dream analyses, and a hackathon judge cannot be asked to subscribe. The seminar and von Franz stay on the reading shelf. The judged run still needs two short sources we are allowed to store.

## Anonymized analyses that are actually public

Jung did publish anonymized dream work. The 1928–1930 seminar is thirty dreams of one man, discussed for two years. Von Franz published dream readings too. Those books are the corpus. They are for sale. There is no open dump of them.

What is free to read are later clinicians, not Jung and not von Franz, writing up a few dreams under a false name:

| Paper | What is in it |
|---|---|
| Matheus Ribeiro de Ramos, "From Identification with the Anima to Dialogue with Emotions" (2025), journal *Self* | Six dreams from a journal kept by "Miguel" over one year, read in a Jungian way. The PDF is on the journal site. |
| Siamak Khodarahimi, "Dreams In Jungian Psychology" (2009), *Malaysian Journal of Medical Sciences*, on PubMed Central | One case, "Lida," and the dreams used in a short therapy. The journal's note asks permission to reuse the text. Hosting on PubMed Central is not a license to copy it into this repo. |

Those are single papers. They are not a database, and they are not Marie-Louise. The Sleep and Dream Database is the opposite: many real dream texts, and no Jungian reading attached. The Frank Miller fantasies inside the 1916 book are the early public series Jung himself analyzed, with the same European copyright question as the rest of that book.
